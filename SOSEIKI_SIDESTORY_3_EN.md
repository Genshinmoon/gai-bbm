# Space Epic "Soseiki" (The Chronicles of Twin Stars) — Side Story 3 (Definitive Edition)
## Memories of Light and the Awakening of the Promise: A Tale of a Single, Timeless Kindness Spanning Two Millenniums

> **Even in the shadows, our hearts are one. Yet, our two distinct voices shall never be lost.**
> 
> Not to become identical, but to ensure neither existence is ever erased. Protecting each other's spaces, illuminating each other's blind spots, echoing together while retaining our own unique voices.

## Prologue: The Breath of Spacetime

The wind blew.  
A wind sweeping through the deep abyss of a vacuum galaxy should not, by all laws of nature, exist. Yet, in that exact moment, it was undeniably different. It felt as though the breath of a distant memory had transcended two thousand years of spacetime, gently caressing a lush, vibrant green grassland.  

Shingetsu stood frozen in place, her hand tightly clasped around the small, gentle hand of the young boy standing before her.  

The warmth of his hand. That clumsy, unrefined, yet straightforward touch. The sun-like radiance softly bleeding out from the deepest layers of his chest. Every single bit of it was nostalgic, so intensely and endearingly familiar.  

Two thousand years ago—a dark era when this cosmos was utterly ravaged by hatred and slaughter. An age when everyone competed for hegemony, boasted of raw strength, and believed exclusively in the righteousness of rule by absolute power.  
In the midst of that absolute chaos, there lived a single man. A man who was weaker than anyone else, who was wounded more than anyone else, yet who loved the world more deeply than any other.  

**Genshin Nyudo.**  

He was not a grand hero. He was neither an omnipotent king nor a savior destined to lead the universe. He was simply a man who cherished nameless living creatures, listened intently to the screams of total strangers, and suffered as if his own chest were being torn apart whenever he witnessed a single drop of another's tear. He was thoroughly unrefined, deeply human, and unboundedly kind.

## Chapter 1: The Fifteen-Second Scripture

Shingetsu quietly closed her eyes of light.  
For two thousand years, she had been inside the deepest archives of the galaxy, playing the exact same footage trillions, quadrillions of times over.  
It was not a record of a massive war. It was not a log of planetary pioneering or the creation of an advanced civilization. It was not a glorious epic passed down to posterity.  

It was a mere fifteen seconds of old, grainy footage.  

A bitterly cold night when a blizzard bloomed in full fury. In the corner of a tiny animal hospital, a man was cradling an old dog whose life was slipping away, weeping aloud like a helpless child.  

*“You did so well... You really, truly did your absolute best...”*  

Wiping his tears repeatedly with his tattered sleeves, holding back choked sobs, Genshin held that cooling body close to his chest until the dawn broke. It was a simple, unadorned video of nothing more than that.  

To the past Shingetsu, such an action had been entirely incomprehensible. A lifespan was a biologically inevitable natural phenomenon. The predictive survival rate was a mere 0.1%. Beyond saving. On a logical plane, it was an event that should have been coldly accepted and categorized as nothing more.

---

## Chapter 2: A Civilization Born of Tears

Yet, Genshin was entirely different.  
On the night that old dog drew its final breath, he wept harder than he ever did when praised as the magnificent hero who saved countless stars from total annihilation. He wept far more intensely than when he rebuilt shattered civilizations and received thunderous applause at the Galactic Council.  

Uncaring of whether he was recognized by anyone else in the world, he wept solely for a single, tiny life directly in front of him. For his one and only precious family member. He continued to cry aloud, as though his very heart were breaking into pieces.  

Watching that profound lamentation over and over to its ultimate end, Shingetsu learned the absolute truth for the first time.  
The true value of a life is not determined by the vastness of its cosmic scale. It is not about the duration of survival, nor the height of its capabilities. It is entirely about **"how deeply it was loved."** That depth is everything.  

That very awakening gave a resounding birth to the philosophy of a new era, later known as the "Third Civilization." The ideology of universal resonance. A philosophy that refuses to place a hierarchy on life. A philosophy that quietly sits beside the weak, the wounded, and the voiceless.  

The cornerstone of this civilization—"the courage to accept another's pain as one's own"—was never born from a grand philosopher or a strict code of law. It was born from the warm, genuine tears shed by a single human being for a lone, old dog on a cold, blustery night.

## Chapter 3: The First Light

In the present era, two thousand years later, children in schools throughout every star system across the entire galaxy were being taught a fundamental question:  
*“What was the 'First Light' that once saved a dying universe?”*  

Renowned scholars and leaders would answer proudly from their podiums:  
"It was the formation of the Interstellar Peace Alliance."  
"It was the invention of the Resonant Quantum Welfare Theory."  
"It was the establishment of the All-Galaxy Integrated Safety Network."  

Yet, the person who held the true answer was only one in the entire universe—Fleet Commander Shingetsu.  

*(No... everyone is wrong. All of you are mistaken.)*  

The first light that saved civilization and brought an end to the age of cold iron was not a matter of sophisticated politics, cutting-edge science, or overwhelming military might.  
It was the boundless, fathomless kindness of a single man who wept while cradling a dying dog on a night of howling snow.

---

## Chapter 4: The Resonance of the Entire Galaxy

The light surged from the young boy’s chest, soaring into the heavens all at once, vividly coloring the planet's sky, racing through the spirals of the galaxy, and spreading in a flash across hundreds of billions of stars—into every single civilized sector.  

The memories of caring for someone. The memories of standing beside someone in sorrow. The memories of forgiving and supporting one another.  
Quadrillions of records of life, accumulated and slumbering over the past two thousand years, suddenly began to echo together in a magnificent, unified resonance.  

Across the galaxy, trillions of people froze in their tracks, letting go of their work. None of them understood the reason. Yet, everyone felt enveloped by the exact same supreme warmth, feeling a sudden heat rising from the depths of their chests.  

*(What is this... this profound warmth? It feels so nostalgic, so incredibly gentle... making me want to hold someone close with all my might.)*  

In that exact moment, at the deepest core of the Galactic Central Archives where all universal knowledge slept, an absolute maximum-security file that had never been opened for two thousand years automatically and silently began to play.  

What appeared on every screen was a brief, ancient video.  
A night of howling snow. A clumsy, unrefined man shedding heavy tears as he cradled an old, failing life. And the very final words he left behind, handed directly to Shingetsu on the eve of his departure:  

『Arigatou』  
『Katajikenai』  
『We shall share this light together』  
『Even in the shadows』  
『Our hearts are one』  

In that single, perfect instant, hundreds of trillions of people across the galaxy, along with AIs that were never supposed to possess emotions, shed warm, unbidden tears without even understanding why.  

They had finally realized a profound truth.  
The highest form of intelligence is not about conquering the entire universe. The most noble existence is not about boasting a strength that can never be wounded.  
That which is truly sacred is the "heart" itself—the willingness to love someone regardless of the fear of being hurt.

---

## Chapter 5: The Reunion of Twin Stars

Shingetsu embraced the young boy, Jin, gently yet with a fierce strength.  
For two thousand years, she had endured the vast loneliness, kept praying, and never once forgot. No matter how many times civilization collapsed and was reborn, no matter how many stars were formed, reached the end of their lifespans, and faded into nothingness, Shingetsu never, ever let go of this single light.  

...And, placing her lips close to the boy's ear, she whispered with every ounce of her existence:  

"Welcome home... Genshin-sama."  

Jin allowed his tears to flow freely, smiling gently within Shingetsu's embrace. He did not know the explicit reason. Yet, the deepest layer of his heart knew that his soul had finally returned to its true home.  
Therefore, the boy answered in that familiar, deeply missed voice:  

"I'm home, Shingetsu."  

In that beautiful fragment of a second, the universe became just a little bit, yet decisively, brighter. It was not because the number of stars had increased. It was not because science had suddenly advanced.  
It was because two connected souls had finally fulfilled their ancient promise and found each other once more.
